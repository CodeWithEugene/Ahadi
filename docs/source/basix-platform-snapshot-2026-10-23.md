## 3. Track 02 page (our track, verbatim)

**Track 02 · Omega payouts — Global payouts and prize transparency**

Track name on your submission: Global payouts and prize transparency

Build an Omega agent that decides who gets paid from a prize fund, routes the payouts, and keeps a public ledger anyone can check.

**Who you are building for.** BASIX.MARKET runs hackathons and buildathons with builders across Lisbon, Nairobi and Chennai. Winners are judged, placed and paid. As events grow, organisers need prize funds that are transparent and payouts that reach people on different continents reliably.

**The problem.** Prize funds are usually a spreadsheet and a promise. Participants cannot see how much was received, how much was paid, what fees went where, or what was carried forward. Cross-border payouts fail, duplicate, or get stuck, and nobody can trace why.

**Your build.** Two parts that work together:

- A payout coordinator — an Omega agent that applies a published funding rule, proposes payouts, waits for organiser approval, then sends them on Stellar testnet.
- A public prize-fund ledger — a page or feed showing funds received, paid, fees and the reserve carried to the next event, each line linked to a receipt.

Use Raven for Stellar development help. Any NEAR Intents cross-chain leg is simulated with a dry quote, because NEAR Intents has no testnet.

**What Omega does here**

- Role: Payout coordinator that applies the rule, proposes, and executes only after approval.
- Memory and reasoning: Remembers every request and payout; reasons over the funding rule; detects duplicates and failures.
- Tools: Stellar testnet, the ledger store, a simulated NEAR Intents quote.

**Example funding rule.** Release funds in proportion to eligible teams. 20 eligible teams out of a 50-team target is 40%. A $1,700 fund then gives $680 for awards and $1,020 reserved for the next event, before fees. Publish the rule before registration.

**Demo script (3 minutes)**

1. Show the published rule: target, eligible count, cutoff and organiser attestation.
2. The agent proposes payouts: recipient, asset, quote and fees. The organiser approves.
3. Payouts settle on testnet. Each appears on the ledger with a receipt.
4. Submit a duplicate request and a failing one. Show both handled and recorded.
5. Show the reserve carried forward to the next event.

**Showcase checklist**

- [ ] Funding rule published and recorded
- [ ] Approval before any transfer
- [ ] Every payout linked to a receipt on the ledger
- [ ] Failed and duplicate requests handled
- [ ] Carry-forward to the next event traceable

**Boundaries.** Testnet and simulation only. No live funds, no real wallet keys in your repository or on screen.

**HackMeridian note.** HackMeridian runs 25–26 October in Lisbon. If you plan a separate entry there, document your architecture, findings and tests now, and record your baseline.

**Stretch ideas.** Payouts to people without a wallet yet; refunds; a public audit view for participants.

**Best for.** Builders comfortable with payments and ledgers.

The rules apply to every track. Entry rules, deadlines, judging weights and boundaries are in the buildathon brief.

## 4. Track 01 page (verbatim)

**Track 01 · Rejuve.bio — Biomedical research assistance.** Track name on your submission: Biomedical research assistance. Build an Omega research assistant that finds and explains studies about medicines or lifestyle changes affecting a biological pathway.

Who you are building for: Rejuve Biotech is building an AI research platform to speed up discovery of therapies for aging and age-related disease. Its approach unifies genetic, clinical and preclinical data into a knowledge graph, then uses neural-symbolic AI to generate testable hypotheses. Your users are researchers who need answers they can trace and check.

The problem: Research findings are scattered across papers. Studies may investigate different populations, measure different outcomes or reach different conclusions. Researchers need an assistant that preserves these differences and explains what the evidence actually supports.

Your build: An Omega agent that takes a pathway and one focused question — for example "Which medicines or lifestyle changes have been studied for their effects on insulin signaling in humans, and what did the studies find?" For trainees: insulin signaling is the communication process through which insulin — a hormone — tells cells how to take up, use and store sugar. Think of a pathway as a chain of biological messages. Omega should discover relevant papers and return: the interventions studied, with source links and supporting quotations; who participated, what was tested and what it was compared with; what researchers measured and what changed; whether the biological explanation was tested or remains a hypothesis; conflicting findings, reported harms and missing information; a conclusion explaining uncertainty and what evidence would change it. Lower blood sugar alone does not prove which part of insulin signaling changed. Keep the scope narrow: choose medicines or lifestyle changes, focus on human studies, and inspect up to 10 papers.

What Omega does here: Agent loop — Plans searches, calls tools, inspects results and decides whether to investigate further or finish. Memory and reasoning — Saves checked sources, reuses findings across sessions and compares evidence across study contexts. Tools — Public literature search, source retrieval and structured evidence storage/export. Use Omega's native orchestration and persistent memory. PLN and numerical scoring are not required.

Demo script (3 minutes): Ask the focused research question. Show the source-linked findings, explanation, uncertainty and tool execution. Start a new session, ask a follow-up and demonstrate memory reuse. Show conflicting findings and an example where evidence is insufficient.

Showcase checklist: Every substantive research claim links to supporting evidence · Scientific terminology is accurate, with brief explanations where needed · Measured findings and proposed explanations are distinguished · Conflicting and missing evidence are handled explicitly · Omega's decision loop, tool execution and memory reuse are demonstrated.

Boundaries: Use public, synthetic or explicitly authorised data only. No personal health data. This is research support, not medical advice, and the interface must say so. Stretch ideas: Extract numerical effects and uncertainty; identify papers reporting the same study; export the evidence trail. Best for: Builders who like evidence, retrieval and agent workflows.

## 5. Track 03 page (verbatim)

**Track 03 · BASIX.MARKET — Builder and marketplace workflows.** Track name on your submission: Builder and marketplace workflows. Build an Omega agent that moves a builder from verified skills to the right project, and explains every recommendation it makes.

Who you are building for: BASIX.MARKET is a Web3 and AGI incubator and marketplace. Builders learn MeTTa, earn verifiable credentials, ship real software in live cohorts, and own what they build as on-chain IP. It runs on the DICE model: Developers, Investors, Creators and Entrepreneurs co-own the value they create. Entrepreneurs bring ideas; the verified builder pool ships them.

The problem: Builders earn badges and finish challenges, then wait. Entrepreneurs post briefs and get the wrong applicants. Nobody can see why a builder was matched to a project, so trust in the match is low on both sides.

Your build: Pick one platform workflow and do it well: Project discovery — suggest open briefs that fit a builder's credentials and interests; Contributor matching — suggest builders for a brief, with reasons; Onboarding — guide a new builder to their first suitable project; Submission review — check a submission against its brief and flag gaps.

What Omega does here: Role — Matchmaker or reviewer that recommends and explains. Memory and reasoning — Remembers profiles, briefs and past decisions; uses MeTTa rules to score fit and explain each match. Tools — Sample builder profiles, sample project briefs, any BASIX access you are authorised to use.

Demo script (3 minutes): Load a builder profile with credentials and interests. The agent recommends a project and shows the evidence behind it. Load an incomplete profile. Show the agent ask for what is missing instead of guessing. Load an unsuitable profile. Show the agent decline to match and say why.

Showcase checklist: One workflow, clearly named · Every recommendation shows its evidence · Incomplete profile handled · Unsuitable profile handled · Mocked services and the integration boundary described.

Boundaries: Use sample profiles and briefs, or access you are explicitly authorised to use. No real user data. Name any mocked BASIX services in your README. Bar to beat: Venture Route, a previous BASIX winner, used MeTTa rules to route a founder's brief to verified builders and show its working. Stretch ideas: A match score reviewers can audit; credential checks against Open Badges; a brief writer for entrepreneurs. Best for: Builders who like marketplaces and explainable recommendations.

## 6. Track 04 page (verbatim)

**Track 04 · World Tennis — Player and club coordination.** Track name on your submission: Player and club coordination. Build an Omega assistant that finds a player the right opponent, court and time, resolves the clash, and asks before it books.

Who you are building for: WorldTennis.app is a verified tennis platform. ID-verified players, coaches, courts and referees are matched by skill level, availability and location. Players find opponents and doubles partners, book courts and track results in one app instead of ten. The idea behind it: your tennis, your data.

The problem: Today a match takes a dozen messages across group chats, a separate booking app, and guesswork about whether the other player is at your level. Players coming from different countries use different rating systems. Courts sit empty in hours nobody can see.

Your build: An Omega assistant for one focused workflow. The suggested one: match available players to a suitable court and time. It: reads player profiles: level, availability, location, preferences; reads court availability; proposes a match with its reasons; resolves a scheduling conflict; asks both players to confirm before a simulated booking.

What Omega does here: Role — Match coordinator that proposes, negotiates a clash, and confirms. Memory and reasoning — Remembers player preferences and past matches; uses MeTTa rules for level fit, travel distance and availability overlap. Tools — Sample player profiles, sample court availability, a simulated booking step.

Demo script (3 minutes): A player asks for a match this week. The agent proposes an opponent, court and time, with reasons: level, distance, shared availability. Introduce a conflict, such as the court being taken or one player becoming unavailable. Show the agent re-plan. Both players confirm. The agent makes a clearly labelled simulated booking.

Showcase checklist: A proposed match with visible reasons · A scheduling conflict resolved · Confirmation requested before booking · Simulated bookings and payments clearly labelled. Boundaries: Use sample player profiles and court availability only. No real player data. Bookings and payments are simulated and must be labelled as such. Stretch ideas: Doubles matching for four players; translating between rating systems; filling a club's empty court hours. Best for: Builders who want a clear, visual consumer demo.
